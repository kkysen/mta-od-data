# RAPTOR: routing the OD data onto trains

MTA's origin-destination data says how many riders go from each station complex to each other,
by month, day of week, and hour,
but not which trains they take.
This crate routes every OD row onto the journeys a rider could have taken
(trains, transfers, walks) on that day's GTFS timetable, using RAPTOR,
and splits the row's riders across them.
From that, `mta-od-data ridership` counts riders by station, platform, route, line, and train.

How it works, and why, is in [`raptor_design.md`](raptor_design.md).
This explains what it produces and how to read it.

## Running it

```sh
uv run mta-od-data fetch-gtfs                  # the GTFS versions, into data/gtfs/
uv run mta-od-data raptor assign-range --from 2025-08-11 --to 2025-12-31
uv run mta-od-data fetch-platforms             # each route's stops' OSM platforms
uv run mta-od-data ridership --by route        # or any report's own `Produced by` line
```

`assign-range` needs the OD Parquet from `mta-od-data prepare` (`data/mta_od_2025.parquet`).
The cost weights for splitting riders are in [`assign.json5`](assign.json5).

## What `assign-range` writes, in `data/raptor/`

The OD data is an average for each (month, day of week),
so each one is routed on a single representative date:
an ordinary date in that month with that weekday,
with no holiday on it or the next date, and no clock change
(see "representative-date rule" in [`raptor_design.md`](raptor_design.md)).
August to December 2025 is 25 dates, Monday to Friday.

- **`manifest.csv`**: one row per routed date.
  - `date`, and the `year`, `month`, and `day_of_week` of the OD data it stands for.
  - `days`: how many dates in the range it stands for (October 2025's Wednesday stands for 5),
    so an average over the range weights each date by it.
  - `feed`: the GTFS version routed on.
  - `paths`, `path_texts`, `trips`: the date's files, below.
  - `riders_in`, `riders_assigned`: the OD riders, and those routed onto a journey.
  - `unassigned_no_stops`, `unassigned_unreachable`, `unassigned_no_departure`:
    the rest, by why, normally a few riders a date:
    no stop served, not reachable within 4 rides, or entering after the last train.
- **`paths-<date>.parquet`**: the date's riders on each path,
  by (`hour`, `origin`, `destination`, `path_id`).
  - `hour`: the hour riders enter, 4 to 27 on the service day's clock (27 is 3 AM the next morning).
  - `origin`, `destination`: OD complex IDs.
  - `rides`: how many trains the path takes.
  - `riders`: the average riders that hour on that path.
  - `wait_s`, `in_vehicle_s`, `walk_s`: mean seconds waiting, on trains, and walking.
- **`path-texts-<date>.parquet`**: each `path_id`'s path,
  shared by every date whose timetables route alike (25 dates, 6 files).
  A path is its legs separated by ` | `:
  a ride is `<route> <stop>><stop>>...`, listing every stop the train stops at,
  and a walk is `walk <from>><to>`, by GTFS stop ID.
  For example `N R01>R03>R04>R05 | N R05>R04>R03>R01`.
  Listing each stop is what lets riders on a stretch of track be counted.
- **`trips-<date>.parquet`**: every train's riders at each of its stops.
  - `trip_id`, `route_id`, `direction_id` (0 is northbound), `stop_sequence`, `stop_id`.
  - `day`: 0 for the date's own trips, -1 for the previous date's still running after midnight.
  - `arrival`, `departure`: seconds on the service day's clock.
  - `boardings`, `alightings`, `load`: riders getting on, off, and on board as it leaves.
- **`platform-waits-<date>.parquet`**: riders waiting to board at the start of each minute.
  - `route_id`, `direction_id`, `stop_id`, `next_stop`: where they board,
    which tell the platform, as for the reports' platforms.
  - `minute`: on the service day's clock, 240 being 4 AM.
  - `riders`: those waiting then:
    riders entering wait from when they enter, evenly over their hour, until their train,
    and transfers from when their last train reaches the stop, or they walk to it.
    Only minutes with any.
- **`assign-range.log`**: the run's log.

`data/raptor-walk/` is the same run with `transfers: "walk_distance"`,
`nycriders`' rules for transfer times, kept to compare against.

## The reports, in [`src/mta_od_data/ridership/`](../src/mta_od_data/ridership/)

Each is generated by `mta-od-data ridership`, never hand-edited;
its `Produced by` line is its exact command,
and `tests/test_analyze_snapshots.py` checks every commit leaves it current.
Each also has its charts beside it, `<report>-<chart>.svg`, linked at its end:
its rows ranked by each of its measures, every row,
each bar labelled with its number in the table.

Every number is an average weekday over the routed range (Aug-Dec 2025),
with fare evaders added, since the OD data leaves them out
(each date's riders over 1 less that quarter's evasion rate).
Most reports have two columns of riders:

- **Aug-Dec 2025**: as in the OD data.
- **2019**: as if the OD data were scaled to 2019's counted station entries,
  before the pandemic (`mta-od-data scale-od`).

The **Peak Hour** is the hour, by when it starts, with the most riders in the first column,
counted when their trains are there, and **Riders in Peak Hour** (or On + Off) is how many.

Computed rows are cached in `~/.cache/mta-od-data/ridership/`,
so changing a report's wording or names re-renders it in under a second
(see `ridership/cache.py`).

### By place: [`by_complex.md`](../src/mta_od_data/ridership/by_complex.md), [`by_station.md`](../src/mta_od_data/ridership/by_station.md), [`by_station_direction.md`](../src/mta_od_data/ridership/by_station_direction.md), [`by_platform.md`](../src/mta_od_data/ridership/by_platform.md)

From the whole complex (Times Sq-42 St/PABT) down to one platform.

- A **complex** is everything connected inside fare control.
- A **station** is one line's station within it, e.g. `Times Sq-42 St (1,2,3)`.
- A **station direction** is a station's trains one way, e.g. `Uptown`.
  These are GTFS's `N` and `S`, with MTA's own label for each.
- A **platform** is a physical platform, from OpenStreetMap,
  so an island counts both of its directions together.
  A platform is named by its station,
  plus only as much as it takes to tell it from the station's other platforms:
  its directions (`Grand Central-42 St (4,5,6), Uptown`),
  its routes (`Jamaica Center-Parsons/Archer (E,J,Z), E`),
  or its routes each way (`59 St (4,5,6), Downtown 4,5`).
  A route counts at a platform only if a tenth of its daytime trains there stop at it,
  so a route that's local only overnight isn't listed.
  [`direction_labels.json5`](../src/mta_od_data/ridership/direction_labels.json5) fixes MTA's labels
  where they don't fit a route, like the G at Hoyt-Schermerhorn Sts.

The columns:

- **On + Off**: riders boarding and alighting there, in each column of riders.
- **On**, **Off**: those separately.
- **Transfers On + Off**: boardings and alightings that are transfers,
  not the journey's first boarding or last alighting.
- **Staying On**: riders on trains that stop there and who stay on.

For platforms only, how busy each is for its size:

- **Area (m²)**: its OpenStreetMap outline's area
  ([`platform_areas.csv`](../src/mta_od_data/ridership/platform_areas.csv)).
  An outline under a quarter of another platform's at its station
  is taken to be mapped only in part, like Newkirk Plaza's southbound, 51 m²,
  so it has that platform's area instead, marked †.
- **On + Off per m²**, **On + Off in Peak Hour per m²**:
  riders getting on or off over the day, or the peak hour, per m² of platform.
  That's throughput for its size, not how many stand on it at once.
- **Most On Platform At**, **Most On Platform**, **Most On Platform per m²**:
  the minute it's fullest, and how many are on it then, in all and per m².
  That's riders waiting there (`platform-waits-<date>.parquet`)
  and those who got off in the minute before, leaving it.
  There's no crowding: no rider waits for a later train because one's too full.

### By route: [`by_route.md`](../src/mta_od_data/ridership/by_route.md)

Each route's journeys, counting a journey once however many of its rides are on the route.
Routes with express variants count as the route (`6`),
then as their local (`6L`) and express (`6X`) separately.

- **Route-km**: the km of track the route regularly runs, each stretch once.
- **Per Route-km**: riders per km of route.
- **Trips**: trains a weekday.
- **Train-km**: km run by all its trains.
- **Per Train-km**: riders per train-km, how full its trains run on average.
- **Per Car\*-km**: that per car-km, where a car is 60 ft of train, about one B Division car,
  so routes with different train lengths compare
  ([`train_lengths.json5`](../src/mta_od_data/ridership/train_lengths.json5)).

### By line: [`by_line.md`](../src/mta_od_data/ridership/by_line.md), [`by_line_and_route.md`](../src/mta_od_data/ridership/by_line_and_route.md)

Journeys riding a named stretch of track at all, defined in [`lines.json5`](../src/mta_od_data/ridership/lines.json5):
a journey counts if a ride goes between two stops one after the other on the line,
including riders passing through without stopping.
`by_line_and_route.md` splits each line's riders by the route they ride on it.

The lines are every named trunk (Lexington Av, Queens Blvd, Brighton, ...),
plus the 53 St and 63 St tunnels, the Manhattan Bridge, and SAS Phase 1 (57 St-7 Av to 96 St).
Some trunks are also split into their stretches, each counting only its own hops:
Hillside Av and Queens Blvd east and west of Forest Hills-71 Av,
Culver north and south of Church Av,
and the A's Lefferts, Far Rockaway, and Rockaway Park branches.
A trunk with express tracks is also two lines, `<trunk> local` and `<trunk> express`,
for riders on each track over the stretch where it has both:
a ride skipping a station is on the express tracks,
and one between express stations with nothing between them is on whichever its route runs on by day,
the A's express tracks or the C's local ones between Canal St and Chambers St.
The J and Z's skip-stop is on the local tracks.

### By train: [`by_trip.md`](../src/mta_od_data/ridership/by_trip.md)

Each route's most crowded train, from the trips files.
A train is the same across dates if it has the same route, direction, first departure, and stops.
Only trains fullest on their route's usual line count:
between stations at least a tenth of the route's trains stop at,
so not the few rush-hour Rs to 96 St fullest via Second Av,
though an E to Jamaica-179 St fullest on Queens Blvd counts.

- **Train**: its first departure and its terminals.
- **Peak Load**: riders on board at its fullest, averaged over the weekdays it runs.
- **Train Length (ft)**, **Peak per Car\***: its length, and that load per 60 ft car of it.
- **Peak Between**, **Leaving At**: where and when it's that full.
- **Weekdays Run**: how many of the range's weekdays it ran on.

## Caveats

- The routing is a model, not observed:
  riders take the fastest journeys on the timetable as scheduled,
  split by [`assign.json5`](assign.json5)'s cost weights,
  which are neutral placeholders for now.
  There's no crowding, delays, or planned work.
- Routing starts at the platform:
  there's no time from fare gate to platform, and riders enter evenly through their hour.
- An express route running local at night is still counted on the express tracks
  between express stations with no station between, like the A from 42 St to 34 St.
- Only August to December 2025 is routed,
  since earlier GTFS versions haven't been fetched.
